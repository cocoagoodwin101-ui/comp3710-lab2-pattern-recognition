88.2% test accuracy vs 62.4% for PCA+Random Forest — a huge jump, why:

PCA is linear — it can only capture variance as straight-line 
combinations of pixel intensities. It has no concept of "edge," 
"eyebrow," or spatial relationships between neighbouring pixels; 
flattening the image into a vector throws that structure away before 
PCA even sees it.
Convolution preserves spatial locality — each 3×3 filter looks 
at a small neighbourhood of pixels and slides across the whole 
image, so the network can learn local patterns (edges, then 
combinations of edges into shapes) and where they occur relative 
to each other. That's structural information PCA never had access to.
The features and classifier are trained jointly, end-to-end, 
via backpropagation — the conv filters are optimised specifically 
to produce features that help this classification task, 
whereas PCA's components are optimised to explain variance 
generally, with no awareness of which variance actually matters 
for telling people apart.

Small bump around epoch 21-22 — loss ticks up slightly before 
continuing down. This is just normal stochastic noise from 
mini-batch training — each epoch shuffles the data into different 
random batches (torch.randperm), so the exact gradient direction 
wiggles a bit batch to batch. It's not a bug, and Adam's adaptive 
step sizes generally recover from small bumps like this without issue, 
which is exactly what happened here — it dips right back down and 
keeps converging.

the smooth, monotonic-ish decline down to near-zero training loss,
