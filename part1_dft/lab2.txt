The very first CUDA operation in any script pays a one-time tax: 
creating the CUDA context, initialising the memory allocator, 
and JIT-compiling the kernels for operations like complex exp()
and complex matmul. That overhead is fixed regardless of problem size,
so for a tiny N=256 problem, the actual computation is microseconds 
but the setup tax is over a second.

Square wave reconstruction
N=1 (just the fundamental) is a smooth sine wave, barely resembling a square wave yet — makes sense, since it's literally just one sinusoid.

N=3 and N=5 progressively sharpen toward the square edges, adding more oscillation detail in the flat regions — each added odd harmonic contributes higher-frequency correction terms that push the approximation closer to the sharp transitions.

The Gibbs phenomenon is clearly visible in both N=3 and N=5: right at 
the jump discontinuities (t=0, 0.5, 1.0), the reconstruction overshoots 
past ±1.0 before settling — that little "spike" poking above 1.0 and 
below -1.0 right at the edges.Adding more harmonics does not shrink 
that overshoot, it just squeezes the ringing into a narrower 
region closer to the discontinuity. If you look closely, N=5's 
overshoot peak is roughly the same height as N=3's, just compressed 
into less horizontal space.
